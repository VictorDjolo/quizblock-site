Add your own flashcard decks

Turn a CSV, TSV or Anki export into questions QuizBlock asks in its quizzes, next to its own subjects.

QuizBlock can ask your own questions alongside its built-in subjects: vocabulary, exam facts, anything you want to drill. Here’s how to write the file, import it and make cards that work well at the gate.

The file format

A deck is a plain text file (.csv, .tsv or .txt) with one question per line. The columns go in this order:

ColumnWhat goes in it
1The question (required)
2The answer (required)
3 to 5Three wrong answers (optional)
6A fact shown after you answer (optional)

If there’s only one column after the answer, it’s used as the fact. Exactly two extra columns are ignored: give three wrong answers or none.

  • Separators: commas, tabs or semicolons all work. Use the same one throughout the file.
  • Quotes: in a comma-separated file, put a field that contains a comma in double quotes. To write a double quote inside it, type two.
  • One line per card: a question can’t run over several lines.
  • Header row: a first line whose columns say “question” (or “front”) and “answer” (or “back”) is skipped. So are blank lines and lines starting with #.
  • Plain text only: HTML tags are removed, so formatting and pictures don’t come through. Text over 300 characters is cut short.
  • Repeats: if the same question appears twice, the second is left out.

Here’s a small deck you can copy:

question,answer,wrong 1,wrong 2,wrong 3,fact
What is the capital of Australia?,Canberra,Sydney,Melbourne,Perth,It was chosen as a compromise between Sydney and Melbourne.
"Translate into English: la mañana, la tarde",morning and afternoon
Chemical symbol for gold,Au,From the Latin word aurum.

If you write it in a spreadsheet, save or download it as CSV. In Excel, pick CSV UTF-8 so accents come through. A deck can hold up to 1,000 questions, the file must be under 2 MB, and you can have up to 10 decks.

Exporting notes from Anki

Anki’s plain-text export works as it is. In Anki, choose File, then Export, and pick Notes in Plain Text and the deck you want.

  • Leave out tags, and the deck name, note type and unique identifier if your version of Anki offers them: QuizBlock reads the first column as the question and the second as the answer.
  • Unticking Include HTML and media references gives cleaner text.
  • Use notes with the question on the front and the answer on the back, like Anki’s Basic type. Each note gives one question. Cloze notes don’t work in this version.

Import the deck

  1. Click QuizBlock in the toolbar, then Open dashboard, and go to Settings.
  2. In What you want to learn, find Your own decks and click Import a deck.
  3. Choose your file. QuizBlock shows how many questions it found, the first three with their answers, and how many lines it left out because they had no question or answer, or repeated one.
  4. Check the Name (up to 40 characters). It starts as the file name.
  5. Click Add deck. The deck appears in the list, switched on.

How decks mix into quizzes

A deck works like one more subject. Click its name in Your own decks to switch it off or back on; that’s instant.

  • It needs a built-in subject too. Decks are used only alongside at least one built-in subject. With none chosen, Settings says so and your cards aren’t asked.
  • At most half a quiz. Your cards make up no more than half of a quiz’s questions. Map challenges carry on as before.
  • Spaced repetition applies. Cards you miss come back sooner; ones you know, less and less often. See how challenges work.
  • Never to switch a site off. That quiz always uses QuizBlock’s own questions.

How answers are checked

Every card is checked: you either pick from four options or type the answer.

  • A card with its own three wrong answers is always multiple choice.
  • A card without them is sometimes multiple choice, using three other answers from the same deck as wrong options (so the deck needs at least three other answers), and otherwise typed.
  • Typed answers ignore capitals, accents, full stops, commas, apostrophes, hyphens and a leading “the” (or “le”, “la”, “der”, “il” and the like).
  • Small slips count as right: one wrong, missing or extra character in answers of five to eight characters, two in longer ones. QuizBlock shows the right spelling. Answers of four characters or fewer must be exact.
  • I don’t know counts as a miss.

After you answer, the fact appears under the question; without one, it names the deck.

Making good cards

  • One short answer per card. There’s no way to accept alternatives such as “colour” or “color”, so a typed answer has to match the one you wrote. Put the hint in the question instead: “in British spelling”.
  • Keep other punctuation out of answers. Question marks, brackets and slashes aren’t ignored: leaving one out counts as a slip, and short answers must match exactly.
  • Say what kind of answer you want. “Year the Berlin Wall fell” works better than “Berlin Wall”.
  • Keep a deck to one kind of answer, such as all capitals or all years, so the options borrowed from other cards look plausible. Or write your own wrong answers.
  • Use the fact column for the detail you want to stick. It shows every time, right or wrong.

Remove a deck

Click the × next to the deck, then Remove it (or Keep to change your mind). It comes off the list straight away, and new quizzes stop asking its cards.

Where your decks are kept

Decks stay in this browser, on this computer. QuizBlock doesn’t upload them anywhere, and they don’t sync to your other computers with your settings. They are included when you use Export backup, so restoring that file elsewhere brings them along: see backup and sync and the privacy policy.
