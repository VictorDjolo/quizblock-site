How to block YouTube Shorts on Chrome and keep the rest

Shorts are built so that one more always feels cheap. Here is how to block just the Shorts part of YouTube in Chrome, or put a short challenge in front of it, while the rest of YouTube stays as it is.

You open YouTube to watch one tutorial. Forty minutes later you're still swiping through Shorts and can't remember what the tutorial was called. If that sounds familiar, you don't have to give up YouTube altogether. You can block YouTube Shorts on Chrome and keep everything else: the lectures, the recipes, the music, the channels you actually chose.

This guide explains why Shorts are so hard to stop, what it means to block only the Shorts part of a site, and how to set it up step by step.

Why Shorts are so hard to stop

A long video asks you to decide whether it's worth twenty minutes. A Short asks for twenty seconds, and twenty seconds never feels like much. So you swipe, and the next one is already playing. The feed has no end and never asks whether you want to carry on.

The clips are also uneven, and that's part of the pull. Most are forgettable, a few are very good, and you can't tell which is coming next. Rewards that arrive unpredictably keep people going far longer than steady ones, which is why a feed is harder to put down than a film. (There's more on this in how to stop doomscrolling.)

Then there's how often you run into them. Shorts appear on the YouTube home page, in search results, in the menu on the left and on channel pages. Each one is a small test of willpower, and you only have to fail one.

What blocking only the Shorts part means

Every Short plays at an address that starts with youtube.com/shorts/. Ordinary videos use a different address (youtube.com/watch), and so do search, your subscriptions and channel pages. That makes Shorts easy to separate: a blocker that understands parts of a site can close youtube.com/shorts and leave every other address alone.

In practice:

  • Clicking a Short, from anywhere on YouTube, takes you to a block page instead of the Shorts player.
  • Regular videos, search and subscriptions work exactly as before.
  • You may still see Shorts thumbnails on the home page, because the home page itself isn't blocked. They just no longer lead anywhere you can get stuck.

You also get to choose how strict to be. You can block Shorts completely, so they never open, or put a small cost in front of them, so they open only when you've decided they're worth it.

How to block YouTube Shorts on Chrome, step by step

QuizBlock is a free Chrome extension that keeps the sites you choose locked until you beat a short learning challenge, or blocks them outright. It can cover a whole site or just part of one. It works in Chrome, Edge and Brave on your computer. Here's how to set it up for Shorts.

  1. Install QuizBlock from the Chrome Web Store. Setup asks which sites to add, how strict to be and which subjects you like. Chrome asks for permission once, and QuizBlock only acts on the sites on your list.
  2. Open the Sites page. Click the QuizBlock button in your toolbar, choose Open dashboard, then go to Sites.
  3. Choose what happens when you visit. Pick Block completely if you never want Shorts, or Ask for a challenge if you'd like to earn them now and then.
  4. Add the Shorts part. Type youtube.com/shorts in the box and press Block site (or Add site).
  5. Try it. Open YouTube and click any Short. You should land on QuizBlock instead, while ordinary videos still play.

Type youtube.com/shorts on its own. If you paste the address of one particular Short, such as youtube.com/shorts/abc123, only that single video is covered.

Prefer one click? On the Sites page, under Add a pack, the Shorts & Reels pack adds youtube.com/shorts, instagram.com/reels, facebook.com/reel and tiktok.com together, and shows you the list before anything is added. For more detail, see Block part of a site.

If YouTube itself is already on your list

A part of a site can only be stricter than the site it belongs to. So if youtube.com already asks for a challenge, you can add Shorts as blocked completely, but not with a challenge of its own (it already has YouTube's). Time you earn on YouTube then opens regular videos only, and Shorts stay closed. If youtube.com is blocked completely, Shorts are already covered.

Block completely, or ask for a challenge?

Block Shorts completely if they're the whole problem and you won't miss them. It's the simplest option, and there's nothing to negotiate with.

A challenge suits you if you enjoy Shorts sometimes but want it to be a choice. When you open one, QuizBlock asks you to find a few countries on a map or answer a few questions, which takes a minute or two. Often you'll find you don't want Shorts that much after all, and close the tab. If you carry on, the time is limited: the countdown sits on QuizBlock's toolbar icon, it only runs while Shorts are open in a tab, and when it reaches zero, Shorts lock again.

If you chose daily free minutes during setup, they apply to every site with a challenge, Shorts included, so your first visit of the day opens straight away. How challenges work explains free minutes, limits and strictness.

Whichever you pick, making things stricter takes effect at once. Going the other way is deliberately slower: loosening a rule, such as switching Shorts off, takes a quiz and a short pause. That way the decision you made with a clear head still holds when you're tired and bored at eleven at night.

Instagram Reels and Facebook reels work the same way

The same approach works for other short-video feeds:

  • Instagram Reels: add instagram.com/reels for the Reels tab. A single reel someone sends you usually opens at instagram.com/reel/ (no s), so add that too if you want both covered.
  • Facebook reels: add facebook.com/reel.
  • TikTok: the whole site is short videos, so add tiktok.com as it is.

The rest of Instagram and Facebook, such as your messages, stays as it is unless you add those sites too.

Habits that make it stick

Blocking takes away the easiest path. A few habits make the new routine feel normal faster:

  • Go straight to what you came for. Bookmark your subscriptions or Watch later page instead of the home page, so you start from your own choices rather than recommendations.
  • Decide in advance when you'll watch. "After dinner, one video from my list" is easier to keep than "less YouTube". Plans with a clear when and what tend to work better than vague intentions.
  • Have something else ready. The urge to swipe often comes from boredom or needing a break. Keep a short list of other things to do. QuizBlock can show yours on the gate: add them under Rather do in Settings.
  • Look back once a week. QuizBlock's weekly recap shows how often you walked away from a gate without unlocking. It counts visits you didn't make, which is a more honest measure than guessing at hours.
